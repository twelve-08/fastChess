How to Play

The Board

Two players, White and Black, take turns moving one piece each. White goes first. Standard chess uses an 8 × 8 board with 16 pieces per side. This game also offers a smaller 4 × 6 board.

How the Pieces Move

A dot marks a square the piece can move to. A ring marks a capture.

King worth: none

One square in any direction. It can never be captured, and it cannot move onto an attacked square.

Queen worth: 9

Any distance in a straight line or diagonal.

Rook worth: 5

Any distance along a row or column.

Bishop worth: 3

Any distance diagonally. It stays on one square color for the whole game.

Knight worth: 3

Two squares one way, then one to the side. It is the only piece that can jump over others.

Pawn worth: 1

Forward one square, or two from its starting square. It captures one square diagonally forward. On reaching the far side it becomes a queen (or a prince, if Prince is on and you do not have one).

Jester worth: 5

Opponent just moved a knight

Opponent just moved a bishop

Moves like whatever piece the opponent just moved, wherever that piece went. Before any move, it moves like a queen.

Prince worth: 5

One square in any direction, or a knight's jump. If your king is taken while the Prince is alive, the Prince becomes your king and loses its knight jump.

Medusa worth: 5

Moves

Gaze: the rook is stone

Up to two squares in any of the 8 directions, without jumping. She never captures. Moving onto an enemy piece leaves her in place and turns that piece to stone for 4 of its turns: it cannot move or attack.

Capturing

Move onto a square that holds an enemy piece to capture it. The captured piece leaves the board. Pieces other than knights cannot jump, so the rook below can take the knight but cannot go past it.

Check and Checkmate

A king that is attacked is in check. On your move you must get out of check by moving the king, blocking the attack, or capturing the attacker. If there is no way out, it is checkmate and the game is over.

Here Black is checkmated. Every square next to the king is attacked by the queen, and the queen is protected by the white king, so the king cannot take it.

If a player has no legal move but is not in check, the game is a draw called stalemate.

Early Tips

  • Move a few pawns first to open lines for your other pieces.
  • Before each move, check whether the piece you are moving can be captured.
  • Trading a piece for one of equal worth is fine. Giving up a rook for a pawn is not.

Checkmate the opposing king before the game clock runs out. If the clock runs out first, the side with more material wins.

Moving

  • You play White unless you choose Black in Settings. White moves first.
  • Select one of your pieces, then a highlighted square. Dragging also works. To use the keyboard, hover a square and press E.
  • You cannot make a move that leaves your own king in check.

Clocks and Scoring

  • The main clock is the game time. At zero the game ends and the material balance decides the result.
  • You have 5.5 seconds to make each move. If you miss the window, your opponent gains one imaginary material point. The penalty stacks and counts toward the balance, but adds no piece to the board.
  • Captured pieces appear in the capture trays. Values: pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9.
  • Checkmate wins immediately. If time runs out, the higher material total wins and equal totals draw.

Modifiers

  • Time-Consuming: Captures add 1.5 seconds to the game clock.
  • Imperialist: Capturing a piece on the board edge expands the board slightly and adds a random piece in the capturing side's color.
  • Avenger: When a pawn reaches the far side it becomes a queen and can revive one of your captured pieces. Choose it from your capture tray. It returns to the square where it was captured. If that square holds one of your pieces, that piece moves to an open square. If it holds an enemy piece, that piece is captured.
